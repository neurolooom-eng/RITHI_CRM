RITHI CRM · Hand stock · process reference

How hand stock moves

Hand stock is derived, never stored. There is no balance column anybody can edit — what an engineer holds is computed, every time it is read, from the movements that put it there and took it away. So this is one document rather than four: reconciliation, returns and transfers are not separate systems, they are entries in one ledger, and the only way to understand any of them is to see what they do to the same number.

The balance, in one line

Six movement kinds, and every one of them is a row somebody can point at. Add the two that bring stock in, subtract the three that take it out, and that is what the engineer has:

MovementDirectionWritten byWhat it means
OpeningINA one-off loadWhat they already held the day the system started
Stock outINStores, on a DCThe approved spare reached them
Transfer inINAnother engineerMoved across from a colleague
ConsumptionOUTThe visit report, or a reconciliationFitted to a machine, against a call
Transfer outOUTThe engineerMoved across to a colleague
ReturnOUTAn MRNSent back to Stores, good or defective

Which makes consumption the control point. Stock out, transfers and returns each have a document and a second party behind them. Consumption is the one movement a person enters freely — so it is the one the database guards, and the guard is a hard refusal rather than a warning.

In · the ordinary way

Stock Out — Stores issues it on a challan

The approved spare leaves Stores and lands in an engineer's hand stock. This is the only routine way stock arrives — which is why the balance can be trusted enough to refuse a consumption later.

The approval chain that gets a spare this far is the Spare module's document. This is what happens at the moment it is booked out.

Reads

  • The approved lines, grouped by engineer
  • The day’s counter, for the stock-out number
  • The signed-in session — who is booking it out

Stock Out · booking it

A batch, a number, and one engineer

spare.dispatch

A stock out is a batch: many spares, one engineer, one delivery challan. The number is generated by Postgres in the same transaction, so there is one way to book stock out and no way to type a DC number by hand — and the challan IS the stock out, one series rather than two.

Who booked it is stamped from the session, not sent by the screen. The name on the challan is the person signed in, and the app cannot influence it.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateOne engineer per stock out. Selecting spares for two is refused — a challan is addressed to somebody.
  • TriggerThe number and the DC are minted by the database, in one transaction, so a batch never half-lands.
  • Only lines still waiting at Stores can go. If somebody else booked one out meanwhile, the whole batch is refused and says how many of the selection are still eligible.

Reads

  • What was approved against what has been sent
  • What was sent against what has been acknowledged

Stock Out · part of it, and the receipt

Sending less than was asked for

StoresThe engineer

Stores may send fewer units than were approved. The balance stays open and the line stays at Stores until the rest goes — so a short supply is a partly-filled line rather than a decision.

Receipt follows the stock, not the stage. A part-sent line has something for the engineer to acknowledge while it is still sitting at Stores waiting for its balance — and a spare counts as received only when the whole quantity has been.

Each stock out can also mark a part refurbished, which travels with the line.

Restrictions & mandates

  • Only the person who raised the request may acknowledge it, whatever permission anybody else holds.
  • Dispatched is Stores’ claim; Received is the engineer’s. The gap between them is stock that has left the building and not been confirmed as arrived — worth watching for exactly that reason.
  • Or Stores may drop an approved part instead of sending it — terminal, needing a reason, and not a rejection: an approver refuses a request, Stores declines to send an approved part.

Reads

  • The opening file, once

In · the one-off

The opening balance

What an engineer already held the day the system started. Loaded once, and keyed on the engineer, the part and the source — so the same figure cannot be loaded twice and silently double somebody’s balance.

Restrictions & mandates

  • An engineer cannot put stock into their own hand. Every IN movement has a second party or a controlled load behind it — which is what makes the balance worth enforcing against.

Out · 1 of 3

Consumption — the part is fitted

Stock leaves the engineer's hands and goes into a machine. It is recorded two ways, and the difference between them is the whole of the next section: the engineer writes it on the visit, or the office books it afterwards as a reconciliation.

Both reduce the balance identically. What differs is who wrote it and what it had to justify.

Reads

  • The call — the UCN the part was fitted on
  • Hand Stock — this engineer's balance of this part, right now
  • Part Master — what was fitted

Consumption · the ordinary way

On the visit report

The engineer

Recorded as part of writing up the visit. The line carries the engineer, the part, the quantity and the call — and source reads Report, meaning the person who fitted it is the person who wrote it down.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerYou cannot consume more than the engineer holds. The database refuses it and says the balance back: "X has 2 of PART in hand — cannot book 3 against this call".
  • MandateA quantity above zero, and a real UCN — checked against the call register, not merely non-empty.

Reads

  • The call register — the UCN must exist
  • Hand Stock — of the engineer named, not of whoever is typing

Consumption · the corrected way

Reconciliation — the office books it

consumption.reconcile

A spare was sent, fitted and never written up. The Spare Coordinator books it against the call directly, without waiting for the report — and the line is flagged: source reads Reconciliation rather than Report.

The flag is the point. It corrects the balance exactly as a reported line does, so the stock is right either way; what the flag preserves is that this was a stock adjustment made by the office rather than something the engineer said happened.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateFour things, each refused by name: the engineer whose stock it came off, the part, a UCN that exists, and a written reason.
  • A stock adjustment with no stated reason is not auditable — which is the whole argument for flagging these apart from the rest.
  • TriggerCapped at the balance, the same as any other consumption. The office cannot book what the engineer does not have either.
  • Its own permission. An engineer cannot write one.

Reads

  • The line as it stands — its original quantity, and who is signed in

Consumption · correcting one

A wrong line is VOIDED, never deleted

The Spare Coordinator

Set the quantity to 0. The row stays — with its original quantity, its reason and its author — and the stock comes back. Reducing it part-way works the same: the balance follows and the original is kept beside it.

Quality records are never deleted here, and the database blocks it outright. A void says both things at once: what was claimed, and that it was withdrawn.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateA reason for the adjustment. Refused without one, in those words: "the reason is kept with the line".
  • TriggerA negative quantity is refused — "set it to 0 to void the line". And increasing a line is capped at the balance, like any other consumption.
  • TriggerThe original quantity and who adjusted it are stamped from the session, not sent.

Out · 2 of 3 — and in

Stock Transfer — between two engineers

One movement seen from both sides. A transfer is a single record that takes stock out of one balance and puts it into another — so it appears as Transfer out on one ledger and Transfer in on the other, and the two can never disagree, because they are the same row read twice.

Reads

  • Hand Stock of the sender — checked as the lines are written
  • User Master — who it can go to
  • Part Master — what is moving

Transfer

From one engineer to another

stock.transfer

A header names the two engineers and the date; the lines name the parts and quantities. The stock moves on the strength of that record alone — there is no despatch and no acknowledgement, because nothing leaves the field.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerA transfer cannot overdraw the sender. The check runs per line and names the shortfall: "Stock transfer exceeds available stock: X would be left with −2 of PART".
  • Checked after each line is written rather than before — so several lines of the same part on one transfer are judged together, not one at a time.

Out · 3 of 3

Material Return — stock goes back to Stores

The part is not needed, or it came off a machine and is defective. An MRN sends it back — and it counts good and defective separately, because they are not the same thing arriving back at Stores and they do not go to the same place next.

Reads

  • Part Master — item code and item name
  • The machine it was removed from, where it was removed from one
  • The visit report it relates to

Return

What an MRN records

stock.return

The MRN number and date, the engineer, the part, and the two quantities — good and defective. Both leave the engineer's hand stock; only the split says what Stores has received.

A defective part also records which equipment it was removed from and the customer, with the report it relates to.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateGood and defective are counted apart. A single "returned" number would make a defective part indistinguishable from an unused one.
  • A defective part with no origin cannot be investigated — which is why the equipment it came off is recorded with it.

Why derived rather than stored

A stored balance is a number somebody can edit, and once it can be edited it has to be reconciled against the movements that were supposed to produce it. Deriving it means the movements are the balance — there is nothing to reconcile, and no way for the two to disagree.

The cost is that every movement has to be right, which is why each one is guarded at the database rather than on a screen.

Report vs Reconciliation

Both are consumption and both move the stock identically. Report is the engineer writing up what they fitted; Reconciliation is the office booking a spare that was fitted and never written up.

Keeping them tellable apart is what lets somebody ask how much of the register is the engineers' own record and how much was tidied up afterwards — a question you cannot ask once the flag is gone.

Void vs delete

A wrong consumption line is set to 0, not removed. The row keeps its original quantity, its reason and its author, and the stock returns.

Deleting it would leave the balance right and the history wrong — and the history is the part an auditor reads. The database refuses the delete outright.
