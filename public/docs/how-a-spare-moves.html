RITHI CRM · Spares · process reference

How a spare moves

From the moment a part is asked for to the moment Stores books it out and the request is acknowledged. Two routes in, one approval chain, one Stores queue. Read the middle column for what happens, the left for which master or record the step reads, and the right for what the system refuses and what it demands. Each refusal says where it is enforced — the form on the screen, an access policy, or a rule in the database — and quotes the words it refuses in. What happens to the part after it reaches the engineer — consumption, transfers, returns, adjustments — is the companion document, How hand stock moves.

Route 1

Call Based — a part for a machine that has failed

The request is raised against the UCN, and the call's own particulars come with it — so the part is traceable to the machine it was asked for.

The cover comes with it too, and that decides the approval chain. A part for an AMC or OGP machine goes to Commercial and then NSM after the manager; a part under any other cover has both of those stages written Auto-Approved when the manager approves it.

Reads

  • The call — UCN, call number, customer, product, serial, the complaint (Complaint Reported, or the Standard Complaint where that is blank) and Item Status (the cover)
  • Part Master — the parts mapped to the call's product, its accessories and the common parts; Show all parts opens the whole master
  • User Master — who the request may be raised for

Route 1 · Raise

Ask for the part, against the call

Request spares

On Spare Requests, press ＋ New Spare Request (or raise it from the call itself), search the call by UCN, call number, party or serial, then pick the parts. One request carries up to twenty spares, each with its own whole-number quantity of at least 1 — and each is decided separately from here on, so a request for five can go forward with three.

Who it is for. An engineer raises it for themselves: the name comes from the login and is read-only. A manager may pick themselves or the engineers who report to them; a role that sees every record may pick any active name on the User Master.

The complaint and the Item Status follow the call. Change either on the call and every spare request on it changes too; ↻ Update from call brings an older request in line. A line already past RM approval keeps the route it was given — only what the request shows changes.

Restrictions & mandates

  • FormA UC Number: "A Call-Based request needs a call (UC Number)."
  • FormAn engineer and at least one part: "Engineer name is required." · "Add at least one spare."
  • TriggerAt most twenty: "A spare request may carry at most 20 parts"; and "Quantity must be at least 1".
  • FormThe part is picked from the Part Master, never typed. An empty list says whether it is still loading or the master has no part for that product.
  • PolicySaving the request and its lines needs Request spares.

Reads

  • The month's counter, one per month of the OR Req Date, written only by the numbering function

Route 1 · On submit

The database numbers it

The request takes an OR number — OR-2609-0041, the forty-first request of September 2026. The counter is one row per month and the increment is atomic, so two requests submitted at the same instant cannot take the same number.

OR Req Date is the day it was asked for, and the approval trail dates the request by it. For a request loaded from the old system it is the honest date: the day the row reached the database may be the day of the upload, and the drawer shows that separately as Entered in the system only when the two differ.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerThe form sends no OR number; the database assigns one when none is given. Once assigned it is fixed: "The OR number is assigned once and cannot be changed", and the same for the OR request date.
  • The stage of each spare is computed from its approval columns rather than typed — so a stage can never disagree with the approvals underneath it.

Route 2

HandStock — replenishing what the engineer carries

No call. The engineer is asking for stock to carry. Everything about the chain below is the same — what changes is what the request must justify itself with.

A HandStock request must carry a written reason, and it goes to NSM: approved at the RM stage, Commercial is written Auto-Approved and NSM is not, so the line stops at NSM rather than at Stores.

Reads

  • Part Master — the whole list: with no machine there is no product to narrow it by
  • User Master — who the request may be raised for, as on Route 1

Route 2 · Raise

Ask for the part, with a reason

Request spares

Same form, Request Type HandStock. The call section disappears and a single field takes its place: Reason for HandStock Request.

The call's fields are saved empty — no UCN, no customer, no cover. A HandStock part fitted later is booked to its call when it is consumed.

Restrictions & mandates

  • FormThe reason: "Enter the reason for the HandStock request."
  • TriggerNSM reviews every HandStock line. Only a holder of Approve spare — NSM may write the NSM column on one; the RM's right to write Auto-Approved there does not extend to HandStock: "RBAC: NSM approval requires the spare.approve_nsm permission".
  • Commercial is auto-approved — with no machine there is no cover for Commercial to weigh.

The register · Spare Requests

One row per spare, and the action it is waiting for

Spare Requests lists every spare line you may see — one row per part, not per order — with its stage, its approvals, how much has been sent and its DC. It is where a request is raised, where Commercial and NSM decide, where a spare is dropped, and where the receipt is acknowledged.

Reads

  • Every spare line and its order, as far as your role may see: an office role sees every request, everybody else their own and their team's
  • The call's status, to colour each UCN

Spare Requests · the screen

Find it, then act on it

the Spare Requests page

The chips across the top filter by stage — ⚡ Needs my action first, then each of the eight stages — and a second strip by engineer; each strip's counts are taken after the other is applied. Search covers the Spare ID, UID, OR number, UCN, party, product, part, engineer, stage, status and DC. Rows group by region, engineer or stage, and export to CSV.

Click a row for the detail drawer: that spare and its stage, where every other spare on the same order stands ("1 at Stores · 2 at RM Approval"), the call it was raised against, and the approval trail — who decided each stage and when, with what Commercial and NSM answered on their forms.

Tick boxes appear for a holder of an approval right or Drop a spare (any stage). A selection is decided each spare at the stage it is at: Approve moves every one on by one step, Reject closes each at its stage, Drop marks each not sent. Anything you may not decide is skipped and counted — "12 spares approved — 2 skipped (…)" — rather than failing the batch. ↻ Update from call works on a selection too.

Restrictions & mandates

  • FunctionA selection rejected or dropped needs a reason: "A reject needs a reason" · "A drop needs a reason". The confirmation names the decision and the count before anything happens.
  • FunctionA selection is refused outright to a role holding no approval right ("RBAC: your role cannot approve or reject spares") or, for a drop, no drop right ("RBAC: your role cannot drop a spare").
  • The count carries a + while more lines are waiting behind Load more.

Reads

  • The order's spares — whether any has been dispatched
  • User Master — the names offered, as when raising

Spare Requests · before dispatch

Change the engineer on an order

Change the engineer on a spare request (before dispatch)

In the drawer, ✎ Change engineer moves the whole order to another name, with a Why. Every change is listed under Engineer on this order — from, to, who and when.

Once any spare on the order has gone out the name is fixed: the parts are counted in that engineer's hand stock, so moving them is a Stock Transfer.

Restrictions & mandates

  • FunctionAfter dispatch: "OR … has already been dispatched — the parts are in …'s hands, so the engineer cannot be changed. Use a stock transfer instead."
  • FunctionWithout the right: "Changing the engineer on a spare request needs "Change the engineer on a spare request"".
  • The Why box is optional on the screen and in the database.

Both routes

One approval chain

The state belongs to ONE SPARE, not to the request. The manager approves or rejects each part separately, so a request for five parts can go forward with three; the later stages read the same per-line state.

The stage is computed, never typed — and only three words in an approval column move a spare on: Approved, Auto-Approved and Cleared for Stores Processing. Any other word — Not Approved, Approval Pending — keeps the spare waiting at that approver, showing the word as written. Reject at any stage and the line is Rejected; there is no route onward from a refusal.

Reads

  • The reporting tree — which engineers this manager may approve for
  • Item Status and Request Type — which of Commercial and NSM will be needed
  • On the RM Approval screen: the complaint, machine, serial and cover beside each spare

Stage 1 of 4

RM Approval

Approve spare — RM stage

Two places to decide it. On Spare Requests, the row's ✔ Approve / ✖ Reject decides that one spare. The RM Approval page lists every spare waiting for a Reporting Manager — grouped by engineer to start with, or by OR number or cover — with Select all N of mine, and ✔ Approve / ✕ Reject for the ticked spares. Spares that are not yours to decide are shown greyed as Not yours and cannot be ticked.

The whole-order "all N" button is never offered at this stage — it is offered at Commercial, NSM and receipt only. A ticked selection is still decided spare by spare.

Both ways of approving write the same. The single-spare ✔ Approve on Spare Requests writes the RM approval and, in the same write, Auto-Approved into Commercial unless the item is AMC or OGP, and into NSM unless it is AMC, OGP or a HandStock request. Approving by tick box — on the RM Approval page, or a selection on Spare Requests — writes the same: the RM approval, and Auto-Approved into whichever later stage the spare does not need. (Before 02-Oct-2026 it wrote the RM approval only, so a warranty spare approved this way waited at Commercial — D-081.)

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerNever your own request: "You cannot approve your own spare request — it goes to your reporting manager".
  • TriggerA manager only within their own tree: "… does not report to you, so their spare is not yours to approve". A role that manages nobody (an office desk holding this right) keeps the wider remit, its own request apart.
  • FormA rejection carries a reason; the stage it was rejected at is recorded with it.
  • TriggerWriting the RM column needs the right: "RBAC: RM approval requires the spare.approve_rm permission".

Reads

  • The line's Commercial and NSM columns — whether each was written Auto-Approved at the RM stage

Stages 2 and 3 of 4 · conditional

Commercial, then NSM

Approve spare — CommercialApprove spare — NSM

Both are decided on Spare Requests, and Approve there opens a form rather than a yes/no.

Commercial answers an Admin Status. Cleared for Stores Processing approves the stage and asks the Reason for Clearing — Under CMC, Under Warranty, Under AMC, OGP or Direct PO; the first three ask for the MC / SA number, Direct PO asks the four PO steps (Estimated Approved? · PO Received from Party? · Invoice? · Payment Received?). Admin Process in Progress records a Pending Reason and leaves the spare at Commercial.

NSM answers a Status — Cleared for Stores Processing approves the stage, Put on HOLD keeps the spare at NSM — with any of the reasons KOL CUSTOMER, LONG PENDING, ENGINEER ASSURANCE, PAYMENT RECEIVED, APPROVED FOR TESTING PURPOSE, an Other, and remarks.

Both may also be actioned for a whole order at once (⇉ all N, when more than one line of the order is at that stage), or by tick box, which approves without the form.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerEach is its own right: "RBAC: Commercial approval requires the spare.approve_commercial permission" · "RBAC: NSM approval requires the spare.approve_nsm permission". Holding one implies neither the other nor the RM stage.
  • FormCommercial: "Choose an Admin Status." · "Choose a Reason for Clearing." · "Choose a Pending Reason." · "Enter the MC / SA number." · "MC / SA number must start with MC or SA and contain no spaces." · "Answer every Direct PO step (N left)." NSM: "Choose a Status."
  • "Auto-Approved" is recorded, not blank, and carries no name or time — nobody decided it.
  • FormA rejection carries a reason here too.

Stage 4 · Stores

Pending Dispatch, Stock Out and the receipt

A spare appears on Pending Dispatch once it has cleared every approval it needs, and stays there while any of its quantity is still owed. Stores books it out there; Stock Out is the list of what has gone.

Reads

  • Every approved line with a quantity still owed, as far as your role may see — up to 2,000 lines
  • The month's counter of the DC date, for the stock-out number
  • The signed-in session — who is booking it out

Stage 4 of 4 · Pending Dispatch

Stores books it out

Dispatch / DC (Stores)

The queue is one card per engineer, longest-waiting first, each showing its spares, units, orders and days waiting (amber from 7 days, red from 14). Search by engineer, order, Spare ID, part, UCN, call or party; 🚚 Dispatch… on a Spare Requests row opens the queue already filtered to that engineer.

Tick the spares for one engineer, set a quantity per line between one and what is still owed, and mark any line ♻ Refurb to issue the recycled equivalent. 🚚 Dispatch asks the DC date (today by default), Courier / carried by and Dispatch remarks, then writes the stock out, its number and every line together — all or nothing — and opens the Delivery Challan.

The challan IS the stock out — one number, SO-2609-0012, not two series. A line sent short stays in the queue as "N of M sent" until the rest goes.

Restrictions & mandates

  • FunctionOne engineer per stock out: "A stock out goes to one engineer — select spares for a single engineer". The screen says so before the database has to.
  • FunctionOnly lines still waiting: "Only … of the … selected spares are still waiting at Stores — refresh and try again"; and never more than is owed: "Only … left to send on that spare (you asked for …) — refresh and try again".
  • FunctionA refurbished issue needs its part on the Part Master and active: "Refurbished part … is not in Part Master, or is not active — add it before issuing it".
  • TriggerBooked by on the stock out is stamped from the signed-in session; whatever the screen sends is replaced.
  • FunctionWithout the right: "RBAC: dispatch requires the spare.dispatch permission"; the button is disabled too.

Reads

  • The spare's current stage — dropping is offered only while it is still open

Any open stage · Drop

Not sent: the spare is dropped

Drop a spare (any stage)

A spare at RM Approval, Commercial, NSM or Stores may be dropped instead of sent — short supply, no longer needed, superseded. On Spare Requests it is the row's ⊘ Drop or a ticked selection; on Pending Dispatch, ⊘ Drop on the ticked spares.

That is terminal but it is not a rejection: an approver refuses a request, a drop records that an approved or pending part was not sent. A dropped line raises no challan.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerA drop needs Drop a spare (any stage), and may not touch the DC: "RBAC: dispatch / DC requires the spare.dispatch permission (a drop needs spare.drop)".
  • FormThe reason is required on Spare Requests. Pending Dispatch asks for it in a prompt and does not refuse an empty one.
  • Already dispatched, received, rejected or dropped: a ticked selection skips it — "already at …".

Reads

  • Every spare issued, one row per part, as far as your role may see — up to 5,000 lines
  • The engineer's address on the User Master, for the Declaration

Stock Out · and its documents

What went out, to whom, against which call

the Stock Out page

Stock Out — and the 📄 Stock outs tab on Pending Dispatch, which is the same list — shows every spare issued: stock out, DC, date, engineer, part (with ♻ Refurbished where it was), quantity, Days to dispatch from the last approval (green under 3, amber from 3, red from 7), OR, call, party, courier and Booked by. Search it; export it to CSV.

Each row prints its 🖨 Delivery Challan — A4, twenty rows a sheet, under the company's mark, identified by Stock Out No. and Stock Out Date — or opens its 📜 Declaration, the sheet that travels with the parcel, which asks for the address, value and purpose before it prints.

Restrictions & mandates

  • Read only. The page is granted on its own on Roles & Permissions and carries no action of its own.
  • A failed read says so — "The stock outs could not be read" — rather than showing an empty list.
  • The challan and the declaration look the stock out up among the latest 500; an older one reads "Stock out … was not found, or you cannot view it."

Reads

  • What was dispatched against what has been acknowledged — the difference is what is waiting to be confirmed

Closing

The receipt is acknowledged

Acknowledge spare receipt

📥 Mark received on Spare Requests, with optional receipt remarks, for one spare or ⇉ all N of the order. Only the person who raised the request — or whose e-mail the request carries — may acknowledge it, so an approver's queue does not fill with other people's receipts.

Receipt follows the stock, not the stage. Each delivery is acknowledged as it lands, so a part-sent line has something to confirm while it still waits at Stores for its balance; the spare turns Received only when the whole quantity has been acknowledged.

The acknowledgement does not move hand stock. The parts were counted in the engineer's hand stock the moment Stores booked them out.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerSomebody else's: "RBAC: only the engineer who raised the request may acknowledge it".
  • TriggerBefore it is sent: "RBAC: a spare can only be acknowledged after it is dispatched".
  • FunctionWithout the right: "RBAC: acknowledging a receipt requires the spare.receive permission".

What every approval cycle writes

Three columns per stage — the outcome, who, and when — and the approval trail in the detail drawer is built from exactly these. A stage with no outcome has not happened; a stage that was skipped says Auto-Approved rather than staying blank. The who on the approval, drop and receipt columns is the name the screen sends; the stock out's Booked by is stamped by the database.

After it lands

Hand stock, and booking the part to a call

From the moment the stock out is booked, the part counts in that engineer's hand stock. Booking it against the call it was fitted to, reconciliation, voiding a wrong line, transfers, material returns, the opening balance and ± adjustments — and the Spare Consumption and Hand Stock screens themselves — are in the companion document, How hand stock moves.

The eight stages a spare can be at

Computed from the approval columns, never typed. The first four are open; the last four are terminal, and the difference between the last three matters — Rejected is an approver refusing, Dropped is a spare recorded as not sent, and Received is the only one that means the whole quantity has been acknowledged.

Why the cover decides the chain

AMC and OGP — Commercial and NSM each decide the spare before it reaches Stores.

Any other cover — the RM approval writes both of those stages Auto-Approved and the spare goes straight to Stores.

HandStock, which has no cover at all, goes to NSM. The two middle stages do not share a rule: Commercial is required for AMC or OGP, NSM for AMC, OGP or a HandStock request. That is why the cover on the call is worth getting right upstream — and why the Item Status follows the call.

Who can do what

Each is a tick on Roles & Permissions, under the Spares pages, and holding one implies none of the others:

  • Request spares — raise one
  • Approve spare — RM stage — bounded by the reporting tree
  • Approve spare — Commercial · Approve spare — NSM
  • Dispatch / DC (Stores) — book stock out and raise the DC
  • Drop a spare (any stage)
  • Acknowledge spare receipt — and only your own
  • Change the engineer on a spare request (before dispatch)

Each screen — Spare Requests, RM Approval, Pending Dispatch, Stock Out — is also a page of its own to grant. Who actually holds each one is set on Roles & Permissions, not here.

Two pairs that are easy to confuse

Rejected vs Dropped. An approver refuses a request; a drop records a spare that was not sent. Both are terminal, and they answer different questions — one is about whether the part was warranted, the other about whether it went.

Dispatched vs Received. Dispatched is Stores' record; Received is the acknowledgement. The gap between them is stock that has left Stores and not been confirmed as arrived. It is already in the engineer's hand stock either way.
