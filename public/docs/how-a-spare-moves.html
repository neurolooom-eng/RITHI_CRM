RITHI CRM · Spares · process reference

How a spare moves

From the moment an engineer needs a part to the moment it is fitted and booked against a call. Two routes in, one approval chain, one stock. Read the middle column for what happens, the left for which master or record the step reads, and the right for what the system refuses and what it demands. Every field named is a real column; every refusal is enforced by a form rule, an access policy or a database trigger, and the enforcement is named.

Route 1

Call Based — a part for a machine that has failed

The engineer is on a call and needs a part for it. The request is raised against the UCN, and the call's own particulars come with it — so the part is traceable to the device it was fitted to, which is the whole reason this route exists separately.

The cover comes with it too, and that is what decides the approval chain. A part for a machine under CMC or WGP goes straight to Stores after the manager; a part for an AMC or OGP machine is somebody's money, and two more people look at it.

Reads

  • The call — UCN, call number, customer, product, serial, complaint and item status (the cover)
  • Part Master — the parts that may be asked for
  • User Master — the engineer, where a manager is raising it for their team

Route 1 · Raise

Ask for the part, against the call

EngineerRM / RGMSpare Coordinator

Pick the call, then the parts. One request carries up to five spares, each with its own quantity — and each is decided separately from here on, so a request for five can go forward with three.

A manager raising it for somebody else may only choose from the engineers who report to them. Raising it for yourself fills the name from your login and leaves it read-only.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateA UC Number. A Call-Based request without a call is refused at the form — that is the difference between the two routes.
  • MandateAn engineer, and at least one part; quantity is at least 1 and whole.
  • At most five spares on one request.
  • The part comes from the Part Master — a typed part is a master entry that does not exist.

Reads

  • The month's counter, one per YY/MM, written only by the numbering function

Route 1 · On submit

The database numbers it

The request takes an OR number — OR-26/09/41, the forty-first request of September 2026. The counter is one row per month and the increment is atomic, so two requests submitted in the same instant cannot take the same number.

OR Req Date is the day it was asked for. It matters more than it looks: the audit trail dates the request by it, and for requests loaded from the old system it is the only honest date — created_at would be the day of the upload.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerThe OR number is minted by the database. The app cannot supply one, and two cannot collide.
  • Status starts Pending, and the stage is computed from the approval columns rather than typed — so a stage can never disagree with the approvals underneath it.

Route 2

HandStock — replenishing what the engineer carries

No call. The engineer is restocking the boot of the car: parts that get used often, so the next call does not wait for a dispatch. Everything about the chain below is the same — what changes is what the request must justify itself with.

A Call-Based request explains itself: there is a machine, it has failed, here is the part. A HandStock request has no such evidence, so it must carry a written reason — and that reason is what the approver is actually approving.

Reads

  • Part Master — the parts that may be asked for
  • Hand Stock — what this engineer already holds, which is what makes a reason checkable

Route 2 · Raise

Ask for the part, with a reason

EngineerRM / RGMSpare Coordinator

Same form, request type HandStock. The call section disappears and a single field takes its place: Reason for HandStock Request.

The call's fields are left empty rather than filled with something plausible — no UCN, no customer, no cover. A HandStock part fitted later is booked to its call at consumption, which is where that link genuinely exists.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateThe reason. The form refuses a HandStock request without one — it is the only thing an approver has to go on.
  • No cover, so no AMC/OGP review. With no machine there is no item status, so Commercial and NSM auto-approve and the request goes manager → Stores.
  • The customer and machine fields stay empty. A HandStock request is not evidence about any device.

Both routes

One approval chain

The state belongs to ONE SPARE, not to the request. The manager approves or rejects each part separately, so a request for five parts can go forward with three; the later stages read the same per-line state, which is what lets them be actioned either part by part or for a whole OR at once.

The stage is computed, never typed — it is derived from the approval columns, so it cannot disagree with them. Reject at any stage and the whole line is Rejected; there is no route onward from a refusal.

Reads

  • The reporting tree — which engineers this manager may approve for
  • Item status — whether Commercial and NSM will be needed

Stage 1 of 4

RM Approval

spare.approve_rm

The manager decides each spare on its own — never in bulk across a whole OR. That is deliberate: the later stages allow a whole-OR action, this one does not, because this is where the judgement about whether a part is needed actually happens.

Approving here is also where the chain is decided: if the item is not AMC or OGP, the Commercial and NSM columns are stamped Auto-Approved in the same write and the line goes straight to Stores.

Restrictions & mandates

  • A manager approves their own team's requests, and never their own. Their request goes to their manager. Enforced in the database as well as the screen.
  • No bulk approval at this stage.
  • MandateA rejection carries a reason, and the stage it was rejected at is recorded with it.

Reads

  • Item status — AMC or OGP only

Stages 2 and 3 of 4 · conditional

Commercial, then NSM

spare.approve_commercialspare.approve_nsm

These two run only when the item is AMC or OGP — the covers where the part is chargeable to somebody. On WGP and CMC they are stamped Auto-Approved at the RM stage and nobody waits.

Both may be actioned for a whole OR at once: by this point the question is commercial rather than technical, and it is the same answer for every line on the order.

Restrictions & mandates

  • Each is its own permission. Holding one does not imply the other, and neither implies the RM stage.
  • "Auto-Approved" is recorded, not blank. A skipped stage says it was skipped — an empty column would read as "not yet done" for ever.
  • MandateA rejection carries a reason here too.

Reads

  • The approved lines, grouped by engineer
  • The day's counter for the stock-out number

Stage 4 of 4

Stores books it out

spare.dispatch

A stock out is a batch: many spares, one engineer, one delivery challan. The number is generated by Postgres, so there is one way to book stock out and no way to type a DC number by hand.

The challan IS the stock out — one series, not two. Stores may also send fewer than were approved; the balance stays open and the line stays at Stores until the rest goes.

Or Stores may drop a part instead of sending it — short supply, no longer needed, superseded. That is terminal but it is not a rejection: an approver refuses a request, Stores drops an approved part, and the two must not read alike.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerThe stock-out number and the DC are minted by the database, in one transaction, so a batch never half-lands.
  • MandateA drop carries a reason. Stock that went nowhere with no reason stated is not auditable.
  • Dropping is its own permission, separate from dispatching.

Reads

  • What was dispatched against what has been acknowledged — the difference is what is outstanding

Closing

The engineer acknowledges receipt

spare.receive

The parts reached the field. Only the person who raised the request may acknowledge it — so an approver's queue does not fill with other people's receipts.

Receipt follows the stock, not the stage. A partly-sent line has something to acknowledge while it is still sitting at Stores waiting for its balance, and a spare counts as received only when the whole quantity has been.

Restrictions & mandates

  • Somebody else's request cannot be acknowledged, whatever permission the reader holds.
  • Acknowledging closes the request — and puts the part into that engineer's hand stock, which is where the next section starts.

What every approval cycle writes

Three columns per stage — the outcome, who, and when — and the audit trail in the detail drawer is built from exactly these. A stage with no outcome has not happened; a stage that was skipped says Auto-Approved rather than staying blank.

After it lands

Hand stock, and booking the part to a call

Hand stock is derived, never stored. There is no balance column anybody can edit — it is computed every time it is read:

on hand = stock out + transfers in − consumed − transfers out − returns

Which makes consumption the control point. It is the only movement a person enters freely, so it is the one the database guards.

Reads

  • Hand Stock — this engineer's balance of this part, at this moment
  • The call — the UCN the part was fitted on

Stock · out

The part is fitted, and booked against the call

Engineer, on the visitSpare Coordinator, reconciling

Recorded on the visit as part of the report, or booked by hand afterwards by the Spare Coordinator where something was missed. Either way the line names the engineer whose stock it came off, the part, the quantity and the call.

A wrong line is voided, never deleted: the quantity goes to zero, the row is kept with its original quantity, reason and author, and the stock comes back.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerYou cannot consume more than the engineer holds. The database refuses it and says the balance: "X has 2 of PART in hand — cannot book 3 against this call".
  • TriggerA hand-booked reconciliation needs the engineer, the part, a real UCN and a written reason — each refused separately by name. A stock adjustment with no stated reason is not auditable, which is the point of flagging these apart.
  • Quality records are never deleted. The correction is a void, and it keeps what was originally claimed.

Reads

  • Hand Stock of both engineers, for a transfer
  • Part Master — item code and name on a return

Stock · the other three movements

Transfers, returns, and the opening balance

stock.transferstock.return

Transfer moves stock engineer to engineer — out of one balance and into the other, one movement seen from both sides. Material Return (MRN) sends it back to Stores, and it counts good and defective separately, because they are not the same thing arriving back.

Opening stock is what an engineer already held when the system started, keyed so the same figure cannot be loaded twice for one engineer and part.

Restrictions & mandates

  • A return records which equipment it was removed from and the report it relates to, where there is one — a defective part with no origin cannot be investigated.
  • The engineer corrects nothing about the stock itself: the Spare Coordinator corrects the stock, not the engineer. A reported line is capped the same way as any other.

Why the cover decides the chain

WGP and CMC — the part is covered, so the only question is whether it is needed, and the manager answers that. Commercial and NSM are stamped Auto-Approved and nobody waits.

AMC and OGP — the part is chargeable to somebody, so two more people look at it before it leaves the building. That is the whole rule, and it is why the cover on the call is worth getting right upstream.

Who can do what

Every stage is its own permission, and holding one implies none of the others:

  • spare.request — raise one
  • spare.approve_rm — the manager stage, bounded by the reporting tree
  • spare.approve_commercial · spare.approve_nsm
  • spare.dispatch — book stock out and raise the DC
  • spare.drop — decline to send an approved part
  • spare.receive — acknowledge, and only your own
  • stock.transfer · stock.return · consumption.reconcile

Who actually holds each one is set in Roles & Permissions, not here — this page names the rights, and that screen is where they live.

Two pairs that are easy to confuse

Rejected vs Dropped. An approver refuses a request; Stores drops an approved part. Both are terminal, both need a reason, and they answer different questions — one is about whether the part was warranted, the other about whether it was available.

Dispatched vs Received. Dispatched is Stores' claim; Received is the engineer's. The gap between them is stock that has left the building and not been confirmed as arrived, and it is worth watching for exactly that reason.
