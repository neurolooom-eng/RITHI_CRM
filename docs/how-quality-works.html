RITHI CRM · Quality & Analytics · process reference

How quality works

A complaint becomes quality evidence in three movements. Every field call is reviewed in the Daily Complaint Review Register; a review that finds a potential effect raises a Field Failure Report without anybody having to remember to; and the reviews and reports are then counted — into the Objective, Product Failure Analysis, KPI & Failure Analysis and Spare Insights. Read the middle column for what happens, the left for which record or rule the step reads, and the right for what the system refuses and what it demands. Where a rule is a target that is not yet fully built, this page says so.

Flow 1

The review — every field call, in three stages

The Daily Complaint Review Register (R/SER/35) holds one review per field call. Review 1 is the vigilance answer taken at registration; Review 2 asks whether the failure has a potential effect; Review 3 codes it. Any Potential Effect, Action Taken and the Review Status are worked out by the database from the answers, never typed.

Tabs: the Register, the Review Desk, three worklists (To be Reviewed, Review 2 Pending, Review 3 Pending) and Export. The register opens on this year.

Reads

  • Standard Complaint master — the complaint is chosen, never typed
  • The call — its three vigilance answers

Flow 1 · Step 01

The call arrives with its complaint and Review 1

Hotline

A field call is registered with a Standard Complaint picked from its master. That value is what every count, filter and frequent-failure match downstream runs on, which is why it takes no free text on any screen.

Review 1 — Public Health Threat? Death? Serious Incident? — is answered on the call itself, so its date is the call's registration date. The review shows it back read-only; to change it, the call is edited in the Field Call Register.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateA Standard Complaint from the master. An empty master is reported as empty; the field stays a picker.
  • A call stays Review 1 Pending until all three vigilance answers are recorded on it.
  • The register lists field calls; installation and PM calls are not reviewed here.

Reads

  • The call card — number, date, customer, product · serial, engineer, status, complaint
  • Age at failure — complaint date (else registration date) less warranty start
  • Visits, newest first, with the hour meter, software version and the signed service report
  • Spares consumed on the call
  • Machine History, in a pop-up

Flow 1 · Step 02

Review 2 — does the failure have a potential effect?

Reviewer · review.edit

Three YES/NO questions: Risk to Patient / Any Clinical Impact, Warranty Failure (1 yr) and Frequent Failure. The evidence sits beside them, so the reviewer does not leave the review to find it. Under Warranty Failure the machine's age is shown, flagged WITHIN THE FIRST YEAR below 366 days and stated as not known where no warranty start is recorded.

Any Potential Effect is shown as the answers are chosen: YES if any of the three is YES, NO when all three are NO, nothing until all three are given. When it reads YES the screen says a Field Failure Report is to be raised — before anything is saved. All NO fills the three boxes in one click but does not save them; the reviewer reads them and saves.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateAll three answers before Review 2 is complete. Two answered is a stage still pending.
  • PolicyOnly on a call you can see (0342): a review — and the Field Failure Report a Yes raises — is saved only on a call that exists and is one of yours, your team's, or every call if your role sees everything. The DCCR Register upload still loads reviews of history.
  • PolicyWriting a review needs review.edit. Without it the register, desk and drawer are read-only and say which permission is missing; the database refuses the write regardless.
  • The answer pickers commit on a click or Enter only — a stray keystroke never chooses an answer.

Reads

  • Every call on the same product, regardless of who may see it
  • Spares fitted, by part code
  • Admin Config — the window, threshold and switch for each rule

Flow 1 · Step 03 · automatic

The frequent-failure verdict, computed in the database

The database

Beside the Frequent Failure question the register shows its own verdict, worked out by frequent_failure(). Rule 1 — this machine again: earlier calls on the same product and serial within the window — always where the same part was fitted, otherwise on the same complaint (or on any complaint, if Admin Config switches that requirement off) — counted including the call under review. Defaults: one month, threshold two.

Rule 2 — the same fault across the fleet: the same complaint on different serial numbers of one product within 30 days. It counts distinct serials, not calls, so five visits to one machine stay rule 1's finding. Either rule makes it a frequent failure, and the screen says which fired, the counts, the threshold and the earlier UCNs behind it.

The verdict informs; the reviewer still answers. Cancelled calls are not counted, and the window runs back from the call's own date.

Restrictions & mandates

  • A call with no serial reads "its machine cannot be identified" — never a confident "no earlier failure".
  • Rule settings are changed on Admin Config; the database refuses a change from anyone but an administrator or a holder of config.manage.
  • Answers already recorded are not re-based when a rule or its settings change.
  • Not yet in place: a change to the frequent-failure rules is not itself recorded with its old and new value (D-067).

Reads

  • Age at failure of each selected call
  • The Auto Review switch — on or off, and in whose name
  • The reviewer's own Auto save choice, over the organisation default

Flow 1 · Step 04

Faster paths that must leave the same record

Reviewer · review.editAdmin · NSM · Technical Support · review.auto

Mark N as NO. Tick calls, or Select all eligible, and answer Review 2 = NO for all of them after a confirmation that says how many. Only calls whose Review 2 is unanswered and whose age at failure is known and 366 days or more are eligible.

Auto save. A personal switch: answers are written a moment after they are chosen and the screen acknowledges each save. The person recorded is whoever's session wrote the answers.

Auto Review. A switch at the top of the register, which always shows whether it is on and in whose name. It starts off. While it is on, from 9:15 am (India time) each day Review 2 is answered NO on field calls logged before that day that failed outside their first year and whose Review 2 is still empty — in the name of the person who switched it on, and marked as an auto-review answer so Review 3 can tell it from one given by looking at the call.

Restrictions & mandates

  • FunctionThe bulk answer skips a first-year failure, an unknown age and an already-answered Review 2, whatever the screen sends, and is written to the audit trail.
  • FunctionOnly a holder of review.auto can switch Auto Review; the database refuses anyone else, and every switch is kept with who and when.
  • Auto Review never answers a first-year failure, an unknown age, or a Review 2 a person has started.
  • Switching it on is refused to a sign-in with no name on the User Master.

Reads

  • DCCR Complaint Grouping and Root Cause Key Word masters, tagged per product
  • Product Master — the product lines, for Change product?

Flow 1 · Step 05

Review 3 — coding the failure

Reviewer · review.edit

Complaint Grouping, Root Cause Key Word and Spare / Consumable / Correction / Calibration, each a pick list. The lists offer the values tagged for the call's product, or every product's where it has no list of its own, and say which. A value already recorded that has since left the list is kept and shown, not cleared.

Change product? — where what failed is an accessory logged against the machine it is fitted to, the reviewer names the real product line. The failure is then counted against that product and not the machine; the call itself is not changed. Blank is the normal answer.

Service Dept Observation is free text. Action Taken reads FFR Generation while Any Potential Effect is YES, until the report number is entered over it.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateAll three codes before Review 3 is complete.
  • A value not on a list is added on Masters → All Masters, not typed into the review.
  • TriggerAction Taken is filled with FFR Generation by the database when the effect turns YES and the box is empty.

Reads

  • The signed-in session and the person's User Master name
  • For a historical load, the file's reviewer names and dates

Flow 1 · Step 06 · automatic

Who reviewed it, and when, is the database's to say

The databasereview.correct_date

When a stage becomes complete, the database records who completed it from the signed-in session — on every path, Save, auto save and the bulk answer alike — and dates the stage that day. A later edit does not reassign the reviewer: the person who answered is the reviewer, not whoever last corrected a spelling.

Old reviews loaded through Bulk Uploads → DCCR Register come in as they were: the file's reviewer names and dates are kept, the person loading is not recorded as the reviewer, a date the file leaves blank stays blank, every row is marked imported, and no Field Failure Report is raised. Re-loading a corrected file updates the same calls, matched on the UCN.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerCorrecting the Review 2 or Review 3 completion date needs review.correct_date; the database refuses anyone else. Holding review.edit is not enough.
  • TriggerOnly a holder of bulk.upload can mark a review imported — it switches off the report rule for that call.
  • Not yet in place: a corrected date is recorded only by the screen's own audit entry, not by the database (D-020).

Flow 2

The Field Failure Report

The Field Failure Register holds the failures that go back to manufacturing, on the controlled form R-SER-03. A report is numbered FFR - 001/26, restarting each year. It has two tabs — Register (as a Desk or a Table) and Insights — and both are narrowed by the same Year and Product filters, so the two cannot disagree about the period.

A report is a record, not an opinion. It is never deleted, and if the review that raised it later says NO, the report still stands and shows as withdrawn.

Reads

  • The review — its three Review 2 answers and its reviewers
  • The call — customer, place, product, serial, cover, installation date, problem, call type, status
  • Its visits, newest first, and its spares, voided lines excluded

Flow 2 · Step 01 · automatic

A potential effect raises the report by itself

The database

When a review is saved with all three Review 2 answers given and any one of them YES, the database raises a Field Failure Report for that call. It is dated the day Review 2 was completed — and its number takes that date's year — filled from the call, its visits and its spares, read from the base call tables so the reviewer's own visibility cannot stop it being raised.

It starts Open, carries which answer was YES and the reviewer's name (Review 3's, else Review 2's), and its CAPA responsibility, CAPA No and CAPA status are left blank for whoever handles the CAPA. While the report's Service Dept Observation is blank, the one written in Review 3 is copied onto it; an observation already on the report is never overwritten.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerOne report per call. A further save, a correction or a second YES on a call that already has a report raises nothing.
  • A review loaded from a historical file raises nothing.
  • A later change of the review to NO does not delete, cancel or alter the report.

Reads

  • The review row, when raised from the review — call, visits, spares, observation
  • Reports already raised on the call

Flow 2 · Step 02

Or a person raises it by hand

ffr.manage

🧪 Raise FFR on the review opens the register's form already filled from the review and the call, and warns where the call is not yet solved. Above it the review lists any report Already reported on the call: a machine can fail twice, so raising another is allowed — it is a decision, not a slip. ＋ Raise FFR on the register opens a blank form.

A report raised by hand is numbered and stamped the same way, and its CAPA fields start blank too.

Restrictions & mandates

  • PolicyBoth buttons are offered only to holders of ffr.manage, and the database refuses an insert from anybody else.
  • MandateProblem reported by customer and Customer Name. Today the form alone refuses a report without them; the database does not yet (D-027).

Reads

  • The year's counter, moved up to the highest number already on record for that year
  • The signed-in session

Flow 2 · Step 03 · automatic

The database numbers it and records who raised it

The database

On save the report takes the next FFR - NNN/YY. The number is the report's identity: it is shown on the form as issued once, never edited, and an update cannot change it.

Raised By is set once, when the report is raised, and the database stamps the raiser's account and keeps it through every later edit. A report loaded from an old year's register carries no raiser, because the sheet's name has no account behind it.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerThe number cannot be supplied on an edit or changed by one; the raiser's account cannot be changed.
  • Old years loaded through Bulk Uploads stay marked as migrated, and cannot disturb the current year's number.

Reads

  • The call as it stands now — status, engineer, visits, spares, the live review answers
  • CAPA status list — Not required · Open · In-Progress · Closed · TBD

Flow 2 · Step 04

Completing it, and the weekly review

ffr.manage

The report is completed in its drawer: the failure (problem, additional problem, observation, problem status, visit remarks, spares), then CAPA and closure — responsibility, CAPA No, CAPA status, FFR Status (Open, Closed or Cancelled), verified by and remarks.

The Desk shows reports on the left and, beside the report, the call as it stands now with its visits and spares. Its chips are All, Open and Due a review — an Open report not reviewed in the last 7 days.

Not yet in place: the drawer has no field for the weekly review date or the attachment, so a report cannot leave Due a review from this screen (D-027).

Restrictions & mandates

  • PolicyEditing needs ffr.manage; the database refuses anybody else. Edit / weekly review is shown on the Desk to everyone and does nothing without it.
  • An edit sends only the report's own columns — never the live call shown beside it.

Reads

  • R-SER-03 Rev 02 — one form definition for the printed page and the Word copy
  • Your saved signature, from My Profile

Flow 2 · Step 05

Printing and signing R-SER-03

The raiserAny reader of the register

🖨 Print opens the report as the controlled A4 form, with Print, a Word copy and Back to the register; 📄 Word downloads the same form. The Word download is written to the audit trail.

A saved signature is printed only in the block that names the person producing the document — the raiser, matched exactly on name or e-mail. Anybody else printing the same report gets an empty block to sign by hand.

Restrictions & mandates

  • PolicyA signature is readable and writable by its owner alone — no manager, no administrator.
  • Row-level security decides which report the print page can find.
  • Not yet in place: the print page sits outside the module guard, so a signed-in user without the register can reach, by its address, any report row-level security lets them read (D-026).

Reads

  • The update log, written by the database
  • The live review — whether it still says YES

Flow 2 · Step 06

It is never deleted, and every change is kept

The database

Every change to a report is recorded by the database: only the fields that differed, from and to, with who and when. The Update log is shown on the report.

A report raised on Any Potential Effect = YES whose review now reads otherwise shows — withdrawn beside it. The withdrawal is itself the thing worth seeing; a report no longer pursued is set to Closed or Cancelled, never removed.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerNo report can be deleted.
  • The update log cannot be written, edited or removed through the application; reading it needs ffr.manage or administration.
  • Policyffr.view reads the whole register, with the visits and spares behind each report. Without it a reader sees reports on calls they may see, or reports they raised — and an empty register says it may be their access.

Flow 3

Counting and analysis

The reviews and reports are read back in four places. Each is computed from the service record rather than kept separately — and each answers only over the rows its reader may see. The module key opens the screen; the read policies decide the rows.

Reads

  • The year's objectives — process, parameter, target, frequency, responsible
  • The Field Failure Register, for the FFR count
  • A cut-off date per month

Flow 3 · Step 01

Objective — the month-by-month figures

objective.manageobjective.lock

A row marked ƒ is worked out from the register; the rest are typed. Re-calculate is explicit, never on opening the page: it asks first, writes only the ƒ rows and only up to this month, and replaces whatever those months hold. A row nobody computes keeps exactly what was typed.

No. of Field failures registered in FFR counts the reports dated in the month — distinct FFR numbers, so one report covering three machines counts once while its evidence lists all three. A month with none reads 0. A rate over nothing stays blank, which means not measured.

A quarterly objective reports cumulatively in Mar, Jun, Sep and Dec; its other months read NA, not zero. Each month has its own cut-off date; a blank one measures to the end of that month. The evidence behind a computed month downloads as a workbook.

Restrictions & mandates

  • PolicyTyping a figure, re-calculating, setting a cut-off and adding or editing an objective need objective.manage.
  • FunctionWhile the cut-offs are locked, only a holder of objective.lock may change one, or unlock them.
  • Not yet in place: the database does not yet record each figure change with its old value, and an objective can be deleted with its twelve figures after a confirmation (D-021).

Reads

  • The review register, built over the field calls
  • The product the review records, where Change product? named one

Flow 3 · Step 02

Product Failure Analysis — what fails, and why

Opened by its module key

Failures are counted under the product the review says actually failed, so a fault moved to an accessory counts there and not against the machine it was logged on. It opens on this year, and a failure's year is when the machine failed — its complaint date, else its registration date. Every year warns that migrated history is included.

Cards for failures reviewed, any potential effect, risk to patient, failed in warranty, frequent failure and product corrected; then ranked charts — by product, cover, root cause, grouping, standard complaint, spare category, repeat machines, software version and age at failure — each with its table. Click a bar and every chart narrows. ＋ New chart builds and keeps your own.

Restrictions & mandates

  • It reads up to 8,000 reviews; a figure over a capped read carries a +.
  • PolicySharing a saved chart with a role or everyone needs charts.share. Sharing a chart never shares data: each reader's chart is drawn from their own rows.
  • Not yet in place: its workbook downloads do not yet test export.data (D-018).

Reads

  • The Field Failure Register, under the same Year and Product filters

Flow 3 · Step 03

Field Failure Insights — whether the CAPA is in hand

Readers of the register

The register's Insights tab counts reports, open, due a review, CAPA in hand, call still open, effect withdrawn, closed, cancelled and migrated, and charts which machines fail, cover, root cause, grouping, customer, report status and CAPA status. Click any bar, slice or point and every figure narrows to it.

Reports raised is a monthly, quarterly or yearly trend; the Pareto drills from Machine to Complaint grouping and Root cause, its dashes marking 80%.

Restrictions & mandates

  • Every figure is taken from the reports the reader may see.
  • Not yet in place: its trend and Pareto workbooks do not yet test export.data (D-018).

Reads

  • Calls in the last 365 days, by product
  • Product Database — the machines in the field, the denominator
  • Spare consumption, by cover, product and region

Flow 3 · Step 04

KPI & Failure Analysis — failure rate per 100 machines

Opened by its module key

The failure rate is a product's calls in the last twelve months per 100 of its machines in the field. A product with no machines on record shows no rate at all rather than one divided by a guess. Cards for machines in the field, calls in 12 months, spares consumed, parts per call, parts on OGP calls and parts under warranty.

Cover is counted in one vocabulary — WGP, OGP, CMC, AMC. Product and Region chips narrow every panel. How products fail lists standard complaints; clicking one opens the Field Call Register searched for it.

Restrictions & mandates

  • A cover value it does not recognise is counted in neither the OGP nor the warranty figure — never guessed into one.
  • The calls are scoped to the reader; the machine count is not — it is the whole fleet, and the screen says so where the reader sees only part of the calls.

Reads

  • Spare consumption, dated by when each line was booked
  • Part Master — consumable or spare

Flow 3 · Step 05

Spare Insights — what was really fitted in a period

Opened by its module key

For a chosen period: spares consumed (quantity and lines), distinct parts, calls involved and the share unclassified; consumable against spare; consumption by the call's cover; the highest-consuming parts and products; and month by month where the period spans more than one.

Both ends of the period count, as whole days in India time. A voided line counts as nothing. A part with no category shows as Unclassified rather than guessed at, and a banner says how much and points to Part Master.

Restrictions & mandates

  • PolicyComputed only from the consumption the reader may see.
  • The unclassified share is never rounded to 0 while any line is unclassified.
  • Not yet in place: the period opens on 01-Jan-2026 in every year, and This year sets the same date (D-023).

Who may see what

Opening a screen and seeing its rows are two different rights: the module key opens the page, the read policies decide the rows.

  • The review register and Product Failure Analysis are bounded by the call policies — calls.view and the visibility rule: your own calls, your team's, or every call for an office role.
  • data.view_all is the grant that gives any other role every record — needed as well as the permission, never instead of it.
  • The Field Failure Register is whole for ffr.view; otherwise the reports on calls you may see, or reports you raised.
  • Spare Insights follows the consumption's own read rule.

Not yet in place

Stated here so nobody reads this page as describing them as done.

  • D-018 — workbook downloads on Product Failure Analysis, Field Failure Insights and the Objective evidence do not test export.data.
  • D-020 — a corrected review date is recorded by the screen, not the database.
  • D-021 — objective figures change without a database record; an objective can be deleted.
  • D-026 — the printable report is outside the module guard.
  • D-027 — no weekly review date on the report form; the two required fields are the form's alone.
  • D-067 — a frequent-failure rule change leaves no record.
