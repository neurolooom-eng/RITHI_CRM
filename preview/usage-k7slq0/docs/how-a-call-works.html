RITHI CRM · Field Service · process reference

How a call works

Two ways in, one record. Flow 1 is the Hotline registering a direct customer call from scratch on New Field Call — the path where every field is answered by a master. Flow 2 is the longer life of a call raised from a request in the field, through the visit, the closure and the review. Read the middle column for what happens, the left for which master the screen is reading from, and the right for what the system refuses and what it demands. Everything here is enforced somewhere — a form rule, an access policy or a database trigger — and the enforcement is named.

Flow 1

A customer rings, and you register the call yourself

The Hotline's own path: New Field Call, with the customer on the phone. No request exists, so nothing is carried in — every fact on the form comes from a master, and the order you fill it in is the order the masters answer. Person Calling is DIRECT CUSTOMER.

The point of this flow is that almost nothing here is typed. Four things are: the complaint in the customer's own words, the complaint date, the three vigilance answers, and the caller's name and number. Everything else is looked up — and where it is looked up, it is locked, because the place to correct it is the master, not the call.

Masters in

  • Product Database — one row per machine. This is what makes a customer selectable: the search offers the ones who own something
  • Party Master — the customer list, so a name on file can always be found even before a machine is against it

Flow 1 · Step 01

Find the customer

Hotline

Type into Party Name. The box searches as you type rather than loading every customer — the machines below are looked up BY this name, so the two lists are run together: owners lead, and the master guarantees a name on file can be found.

A customer who owns no machine is shown, greyed, reading "no machine on record". Shown rather than hidden on purpose: the reason on the row beats a bare "nothing matches".

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateA customer who owns a machine. On a field call there is nothing to service otherwise.
  • The exception is an installation, which reaches somebody who has no machine yet — there, a new customer may be typed in.
  • The customer search must never come before the machine search on a request; here you are the one holding the phone, so you start from who is calling.

Masters in

  • Party Master → Serviceman, read the moment the customer is settled and held as the fallback engineer
  • Product Database — the products this customer owns, then the serials of that product

Flow 1 · Step 02

Pick the machine — product, then serial

Hotline

Two steps rather than one box, because that is how the caller knows their own equipment: "the ORION-G", and then the serial off the label. The serial list is the machines this customer holds of that product.

The customer's Serviceman is fetched once here, when the party settles — not again for every serial. If the Party Master has no one against them, the box simply stays empty.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateThe serial number. A service record that names a product but not the individual device is not a record of anything.
  • Every serial is offered, not the first thousand — a register that quietly truncates makes a real serial read as "nothing matches".

Masters in

  • Warranty Register — warranty number, start, end
  • Contract Register — contract number, start, end, CMC or AMC
  • Cover vocabulary — WGP · OGP · CMC · AMC, and nothing else

Flow 1 · Step 03

What the machine brings with it

Choosing the serial fills eight fields at once, off that machine's row: the city and state, the cover it is under, and its warranty and contract particulars. Nobody types them and nobody should have to remember them.

They arrive locked — the form says From Product Database (locked). That is the whole idea: a cover typed onto a call is true of that call and of nothing else, so the next call on the same machine disagrees with it.

Restrictions & mandates

  • Locked, not merely prefilled. Wrong cover? Correct the machine in Product Database, or the register that feeds it — then every call reads right, including the ones already raised.
  • Cover is one vocabulary. "Warranty" is WGP, normalised on write, because two spellings split every count made on it without saying so.

Masters in

  • Product Database → Service Engineer (the machine's own)
  • Party Master → Serviceman (the customer's)
  • User Master — the list the box can be changed to

Flow 1 · Step 04

Who it goes to

The machine wins. Call Allocated To is filled from the machine's own Service Engineer; where the machine names nobody, the customer's Serviceman from the Party Master answers instead.

Two masters, one box, and a stated precedence — so the common case needs no decision and the awkward one still has an answer.

Restrictions & mandates

  • A prefill, not a lock. Unlike the cover, this box stays editable: whoever registers the call may know the regular engineer is on leave, and the master cannot.
  • What it can be changed to is the User Master — the directory plus the real sign-ins, deduplicated. Not a free-text name.

Masters in

  • Standard Complaint master — the controlled fault vocabulary
  • Past calls on this machine — offered as suggestions, with how often each was chosen

Flow 1 · Step 05

What is wrong — in two fields, doing two jobs

Standard Complaint is the controlled one: every count, filter and repeat-failure match downstream runs on that value. Complaint Reported is the customer's own words, free text, and is the one you read back to them.

Under both, past calls on this machine and this fault are offered — "chosen on 85 similar calls". They state their grounds, you choose, and they appear only while the call is being registered.

Restrictions & mandates

  • The Standard Complaint takes no free text, in any module. A typed value is a master entry that does not exist, and it matches nothing downstream. An empty master is a master problem — the field says so and stays a picker.
  • MandateComplaint Reported, and the complaint date.

Masters in

  • Admin Config — which Hotline desk a call is filed to
  • The signed-in session — who is actually typing

Flow 1 · Step 06

Save — and the database does the rest

Three things are assigned on save and none of them can be supplied by the app:

The UCN — 26I12F0002, the second Field call of 12 September 2026. The Call Number — CL26##### from a running series, which is what a direct customer call gets; a call raised from a request carries the request's own key instead. And both registrant columns.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateThe three vigilance answers — public health threat, death, serious incident. Defaulted to NO, never skippable, and every later change to one is kept.
  • TriggerCreated By is the Hotline desk the call is filed to. Actually Registered By is you, taken from the session and not settable by the app.
  • The two differing is the finding, not an error: only the Hotline engineer is trained on the vigilance questions, so a call registered by a stand-in is visible as one.

Flow 2

The call's whole life, from a request in the field

The longer path, and the one most calls take: an engineer in the field raises a request, the Hotline turns it into a call, and it runs to a visit, a closure and a review. Flow 1 is the first two of these stages compressed into one screen; everything from Stage 03 onwards is the same for both.

Masters in

  • Product Database — every machine on record: model + serial, its customer, its cover
  • Party Master — the customer, and the service engineer who looks after them

Flow 2 · Stage 01

A request is raised

EngineerRegional ManagerHotline

Somebody in the field reports a fault. The request is identified by a REQID the database mints, and each machine on it becomes one call keyed REQID-Product-Serial.

The machine is found first, and the customer is read off it. Searching for the customer before the machine would mean knowing who owns it to find out who owns it.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateA serial number. A key that reads …-NA is a defect, not a variant.
  • One machine appears at most once on a request.
  • The first call fixes the customer for the whole request.
  • A party that owns no machine cannot be chosen — except on an installation, which reaches a customer who has none yet.

Masters in

  • Warranty & Contract registers — the cover on this machine today
  • Cover vocabulary — WGP · OGP · CMC · AMC, and nothing else

Flow 2 · Stage 02

It is registered as a call

Hotline desk

The request is dispositioned — registered as a new call, mapped to an existing one, or cancelled with a reason. A request cannot be silently dropped: one nobody can see is indistinguishable from one nobody made.

On save the database assigns the UCN — year, month letter, day, a type letter and a sequence: 26I12F0002 is the second Field call of 12 September 2026. Installation calls take I, preventive maintenance P, and the three live in separate tables behind one view, with a CHECK constraint against mis-filing.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateCustomer, product, serial, complaint date and the complaint as reported.
  • MandateThe three vigilance answers — public health threat, death, serious incident. Defaulted to NO and never skippable.
  • TriggerThe UCN is minted by the database. The app cannot supply one.
  • Two registrant columns, and they answer different questions — the Hotline desk the call is filed to, and the person who actually typed it in. The two differing is the finding: only the Hotline engineer is trained on the vigilance questions.

Masters in

  • Standard Complaint master — the controlled fault vocabulary
  • User Master — the engineers a call can be allotted to
  • Party Master — the serviceman already looking after this customer

Flow 2 · Stage 03

An engineer is allotted

HotlineReporting ManagerRegional Manager

The engineer is proposed from the machine first and the party second; a call raised from a request goes to the person who raised it. A manager can move a call, or several at once, between the engineers reporting to them and themselves.

Past calls on the same machine and complaint are offered as suggestions while the call is being registered — they state their grounds, they can be overruled, and nothing is written without somebody choosing it.

Restrictions & mandates

  • The Standard Complaint takes no free text, in any module. Every count, filter and frequent-failure match runs on that value, so a hand-typed one matches nothing. An empty master is a master problem.
  • A manager may allot only within their own reporting tree.
  • Every dropdown is type-search-and-select — a native picker commits on the first keystroke, which is how a Root Cause nobody chose was once saved.

Masters in

  • Part Master — what may be requested
  • Hand Stock — what this engineer actually holds, derived: issued − consumed ± transfers − returns

Flow 2 · Stage 04 · optional

Spares are requested and fitted

EngineerRMCommercialNSMStores

A spare request runs RM → Commercial → NSM → Stores, and Commercial and NSM approve automatically unless the item is AMC or OGP. Stores may issue fewer than were asked for when only part of the quantity is available; the balance stays open.

The engineer acknowledges each delivery. A spare counts as received only when the whole quantity has been.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerConsumption is capped at the engineer's hand-stock balance. Hand stock is derived and never stored, so consumption is the control point.
  • A wrong line is voided, never deleted — quantity set to zero, the row retained with its original quantity, reason and author, and the stock returns.
  • An engineer corrects nothing about the stock itself; the Spare Coordinator does.

Masters in

  • Service Manuals — the controlled document at the point of work
  • Call status vocabulary — what a visit may conclude

Flow 2 · Stage 05

A visit is recorded

Engineer

One row per visit: what was observed, what was done, the readings, and the status the visit concluded with. The call's status is then the latest ENTRY's — not the latest visit date, which is a different thing when a visit is written up late.

A call is Unattended only while it has no visit row at all. After that it reads as whatever the last entry said.

Restrictions & mandates

  • MandateEvery part fitted is recorded against the call, without a further confirming step.
  • A visit cannot be dated in the future, nor before the complaint it answers.
  • A closed call takes no visit entry and no spare request until it is re-opened.

Masters in

  • Root Cause and Complaint Grouping lists — the review's own vocabulary

Flow 2 · Stage 06

It is closed, reviewed, and read back

HotlineNSMQuality

A closed call is reviewed in the Daily Complaint Review Register — the judgement that a Field Failure Report is raised from, and the record every failure analysis is computed over. Every judgement carries who made it, taken from the signed-in session.

A closed call can be re-opened by an authorised role where further work or a correction is needed, and the re-opening is recorded and counted.

Restrictions & mandates

  • TriggerA quality record is never deleted. The database refuses it outright.
  • A call reading Solved — Report Completed is read-only to everyone but an administrator. The way back is Re-open, not an exemption — otherwise a call's history could gain a visit that never happened.
  • Every amendment after registration is kept, with the old value and the new.

The status code

These four colours are fixed, and identical in light and dark. A colour people have learned to read is a code, and a code that means something else after dark is not one. A UCN carries its call's colour wherever it appears — in any module. Where a screen does not know the state, the UCN is drawn plain, because a wrong colour on a code is worse than no colour.

Who may see which call

Visibility is decided by the database, not by the screen, and it is evaluated once per query rather than once per row.

  • An engineer sees their own calls.
  • A reporting manager sees their team's, down the whole reporting tree.
  • The office roles — Hotline, NSM, Commercial, Spare Coordinator, Stores, Tally — see everything, because they coordinate across engineers.
  • Anyone else needs the explicit grant. Opening a screen and seeing the rows are two different rights: the module key opens the page, the read policy decides the rows.

Editing is granted section by section

One "edit" right cannot express the distinction a quality record needs — correcting a telephone number and re-answering whether a patient was harmed are not the same act. Each is its own authority, each refusal comes from the database, and each change is attributable:

  • the complaint as reported
  • the customer and the machine it names
  • the vigilance answers
  • the customer's contact details
  • re-allotting the call · cancelling and restoring it

What a call is worth downstream

The call is not the end of anything. Its record is read by the Daily Complaint Review Register, the Field Failure Register, Product Failure Analysis, the KPI views, the consumption reports and the customer feedback.

Which is why the controlled values matter more than they look: a hand-typed complaint, a second spelling of one cover, a status that does not follow the latest entry — each one is a number somebody later acts on, quietly wrong.
